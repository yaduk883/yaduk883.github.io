Parts of speech

Every English word plays one of these eight roles in a sentence. Knowing them makes every other grammar rule — including the tenses below — much easier to follow.

Noun

Names a person, place, thing, or idea.

The teacher placed the book on the table.

Pronoun

Stands in for a noun so it doesn't have to be repeated.

She gave it to him.

Verb

Expresses an action or a state of being.

They run every morning.

Adjective

Describes or modifies a noun.

It was a beautiful sunset.

Adverb

Modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb — often answering "how," "when," or "where."

He spoke softly.

Preposition

Shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word.

The cat is under the table.

Conjunction

Joins words, phrases, or clauses together.

I wanted to go, but it rained.

Interjection

Expresses a sudden feeling or reaction.

Wow! That's amazing.

Articles

English has three articles, and they decide whether a noun is being introduced for the first time or already known to the listener.

A / An — indefinite articles

Used before a singular, countable noun when it isn't a specific one the listener already knows about. Use a before a consonant sound, and an before a vowel sound (not just a vowel letter — it's the sound that matters, e.g. "an hour," "a university").

  • I saw a dog in the park.
  • She ate an apple.
  • He waited for an hour.

The — definite article

Used when the noun is specific — already mentioned, already known, or unique (there's only one of it).

  • I saw a dog in the park. The dog was barking.
  • Please close the door.
  • The sun rises in the east.

Present tenses

Four ways to talk about now, habits, or things that connect the past to the present.

Present Simple

Subject + base verb (+s/es)
  • Affirm. She writes a letter every day.
  • Neg. She does not write a letter every day.
  • Question Does she write a letter every day?

Present Continuous

Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
  • Affirm. She is writing a letter right now.
  • Neg. She is not writing a letter right now.
  • Question Is she writing a letter right now?

Present Perfect

Subject + has/have + past participle
  • Affirm. She has written the letter.
  • Neg. She has not written the letter.
  • Question Has she written the letter?

Present Perfect Continuous

Subject + has/have been + verb-ing
  • Affirm. She has been writing the letter for an hour.
  • Neg. She has not been writing the letter for an hour.
  • Question Has she been writing the letter for an hour?

Past tenses

Four ways to talk about something that already happened.

Past Simple

Subject + past form of verb
  • Affirm. She wrote a letter yesterday.
  • Neg. She did not write a letter yesterday.
  • Question Did she write a letter yesterday?

Past Continuous

Subject + was/were + verb-ing
  • Affirm. She was writing a letter at 8 pm.
  • Neg. She was not writing a letter at 8 pm.
  • Question Was she writing a letter at 8 pm?

Past Perfect

Subject + had + past participle
  • Affirm. She had written the letter before he arrived.
  • Neg. She had not written the letter before he arrived.
  • Question Had she written the letter before he arrived?

Past Perfect Continuous

Subject + had been + verb-ing
  • Affirm. She had been writing the letter for an hour before he arrived.
  • Neg. She had not been writing the letter before he arrived.
  • Question Had she been writing the letter before he arrived?

Future tenses

Four ways to talk about what hasn't happened yet.

Future Simple

Subject + will + base verb
  • Affirm. She will write a letter tomorrow.
  • Neg. She will not write a letter tomorrow.
  • Question Will she write a letter tomorrow?

Future Continuous

Subject + will be + verb-ing
  • Affirm. She will be writing a letter at 8 pm tomorrow.
  • Neg. She will not be writing a letter at 8 pm tomorrow.
  • Question Will she be writing a letter at 8 pm tomorrow?

Future Perfect

Subject + will have + past participle
  • Affirm. She will have written the letter by tomorrow.
  • Neg. She will not have written the letter by tomorrow.
  • Question Will she have written the letter by tomorrow?

Future Perfect Continuous

Subject + will have been + verb-ing
  • Affirm. She will have been writing the letter for an hour by then.
  • Neg. She will not have been writing the letter for an hour by then.
  • Question Will she have been writing the letter for an hour by then?
